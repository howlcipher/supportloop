# Answered Is Not Resolved: case study plan

Status: the separately authorized observational case is published as
[the Blizzard experience case study](blizzard_case_study.md). It has no controlled
empirical results; this plan still governs any later evidence or performance work.
Any future real incident would be an observational example. No raw incident
artifacts, payment credentials, account identifier or screenshot is included
in this repository. The benchmark contains only synthetic
cases and synthetic policies unrelated to any real vendor's eligibility rules.

## Evidence acquisition for stronger incident claims

With explicit material-specific consent, use redacted message exports, visible
status events, timestamps with timezones and relevant public policy versions.
Record source, acquisition time, observation cutoff, known omissions and
redaction transformations. Keep originals local and restricted. Do not inspect
private accounts, mounted drives or private HowlWriter stores directly.

Tag each incident assertion as participant-reported, artifact-supported,
derived calculation, hypothesis, or unknown. A participant's observation
requires separate evidence before supporting a stronger claim. No visible
individual review is not proof no internal review happened. A public suggestion
to submit a ticket is not evidence that an authorized reviewer received it.
Do not infer entitlement, misconduct, damages or an SLA breach without the
necessary evidence and applicable terms. If entitlement analysis becomes part
of the study, separately research current authoritative policy and legal sources.

## Proposed narrative

1. Executive summary with the final measured conclusion, including a stop result.
2. Motivation, observational timeline and limits of customer-visible evidence.
   Include the upstream checkout journey where consented evidence supports it:
   default plan selection, term/price visibility and correction opportunities.
   Use [the UX research plan](checkout_ux_research.md) to distinguish reported
   accidental commitment from inspected UI behavior or unproven design intent.
3. Response completion, decision completeness and objective outcomes.
4. Existing industry QA and the narrower proposed evidence contract.
5. Preregistered methods, independent labels, baselines and adversarial cases.
6. Architecture and implementation only after the gates permit implementation.
7. Actual controlled results, uncertainty, disagreements and error analysis.
8. Limitations: synthetic selection, missing internal evidence and market unknowns.
9. Recommendations for support engineering and AI-driven service.
10. Reproducible demo with versions, hashes and commands.

The real incident stays outside performance evaluation. Do not tune the tool to
agree with its motivating complaint. Any LinkedIn article derives from the
reviewed case study, links the methods and limitations, and invents no result.
Owner review is required before any external upload or publication of the case.
